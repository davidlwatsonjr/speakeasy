English Braille Alphabet – Six-Dot Letters and Numbers

Braille represents characters using patterns of raised dots. A six-dot cell has dots 1, 2 and 3 down the left column and dots 4, 5 and 6 down the right column. This guide introduces the visual patterns in uncontracted English Braille.

How to practice

  1. Learn A–J first. K–T repeat those patterns with dot 3 added.
  2. U, V, X, Y and Z build on the first patterns with dots 3 and 6; W is an exception.
  3. The numeric indicator (⠼, dots 3-4-5-6) introduces digits. The digit patterns 1–0 are based on A–J.
  4. Use the dot keypad in Type → Decode to construct a cell, or practice decoding short words in Test.

A screen cannot reproduce raised-dot touch. SpeakEasy teaches visual recognition and basic uncontracted letter and number patterns, not tactile reading fluency, contracted Braille or full Unified English Braille transcription.
